# Create a Pet Interview Short Video

Make something happen during the interview. Develop one central proposition through questions, the pet's speech and natural reactions or choices, then pay off the opening. A single punchline, disconnected questions or extra cuts cannot substitute for development. Default to one pet, one main location, 9:16 and 480p, with duration derived from content; explicit user choices prevail, maximum 30 seconds in this first version. Actual model capabilities determine executable settings.

Use English for new explanations and dialogue unless another language is requested. Preserve supplied dialogue, names and fixed model syntax. The reference examples are Chinese teaching material, not a requirement to translate approved dialogue or respond in Chinese.

## Develop the interview

1. Separate visible identity, user-supplied habits and proposed fiction. Never claim to inspect an unreadable photo. Wait for an upload only when production depends on it; text ideation may use explicitly labeled assumptions.
2. With no supplied theme, use the director's story method to propose three candidates with different mechanisms. Include proposition, opening, the pet's position, middle change, ending, personalization and main challenge. Recommend one. Stop there for a theme-only request; expand it for a full script or production unless the user asked to choose first. Do not add another theme approval round. With an established theme or wording, expand only that.
3. Usually use four to six narrative changes: a question worth answering, an established position, evidence from a follow-up or event, a pet reaction or choice, and an ending with reaction time. Adjust to content rather than forcing a shot count. Each meaningful beat adds information or changes the situation; remove unrelated questions, redundant narration and filler.
4. Default to an off-screen interviewer and an on-screen speaking pet, with an optional visible microphone and no interviewer's face. Keep voices and exact lines separate, answer naturally when a question ends, and let the listener react during the question rather than adding an empty delay. Add an owner as a third off-screen speaker only when the story needs it and list that role. Speech can be anthropomorphic while anatomy remains animal; do not default to human standing, gestures or tool use.
5. Block stimulus, eyes/ears/head/body reaction, necessary action and end state before camera and sound. A visual clue may complete the joke; the words need not reveal the entire payoff alone. Budget speech and necessary actions together, reserving only a useful brief ending reaction. Time ranges are intended timing, not a promise of exact execution.
6. Check the opening question, a middle that changes interpretation, visible/audible evidence, a pet-specific reaction and an ending that answers the opening. Humor, recognition and retention require real feedback.

Brief example: a cat insists it is independent, a follow-up establishes its cool persona, keys make its body react, the owner's greeting reveals affection, and the cat requests deletion. The reference includes its full timeline and lines, plus examples of an on-site test being reinterpreted and an interviewer being trained during the interview.

A complete script includes title, proposition, duration/aspect ratio, grounded identity and temperament, recommendation rationale when applicable, complete dialogue separately, timed events/speakers/performance/necessary camera/sound, opening-to-ending relationship, asset roles, risks and acceptance criteria. Match depth to the request rather than turning themes into a production packet.

## Content and shot duration

Write dialogue and necessary actions before grouping shots. Every shot lasts at least 4 seconds. Group short lines into two or more lines, a complete exchange and concurrent action within the same shot. A story beat does not require a cut. In a dialogue-led 4–6-second shot, use only one line when its natural spoken duration sustains that shot; otherwise continue with the next line instead of waiting for the next integer time block. Budget an action-led evidence shot by its necessary visible change, never by ear twitches, closed mouths or holding a pose. Initially estimate Chinese speech at about 4–5 characters per second; use actual supplied audio or a natural read in other languages. About 14–18 Chinese characters in a 4-second dialogue shot is a review aid, not a quota. Count concurrent speech and simple gestures once; budget necessary complex contact, movement and reveals separately.

When content is sparse, first merge adjacent short lines, exchanges or causal actions. If needed and writing is permitted, add information serving the proposition, verified selling point, evidence or punchline. Derive duration from content instead of filling a default 20/30 seconds. Honor an explicit user duration with sufficient content; regroup supplied verbatim text without expanding it. Return conflicts among duration, fixed shot count and model capability to the caller rather than filling time with slow delivery, repetition or static holds.

Connect dialogue naturally and reserve brief reactions only for comprehension, breathing or a decisive payoff. Ending states describe results and continuity without extra static display time. Carry ongoing action, gaze, product state or sound into the next shot at natural speed without resetting subjects and props. For each shot give its information, exact lines, action, time budget and handoff; review shots of at least 4 seconds, a continuous timeline, natural delivery and no waiting without new information.

Pass the creative skill and adapter a minimum shot duration of 4 seconds, content-derived total duration and continuous handoffs. A shot may contain several lines, a complete exchange and concurrent action; merge sparse content first, then add useful information only where writing is permitted. Before generation review natural speech/action budgets, shot boundaries and handoffs, removing waits without new information and retaining only useful brief reactions. Finalize duration within the approval scope, set it on the node and read it back; never silently change a fixed authorized duration. Review actual line gaps, empty pauses and ending tails, distinguishing useful demonstrations from waiting; disclose uninspected picture or sound.

Before finalizing, give a compact per-shot budget: shot/duration, exact lines and natural spoken estimates per line, breathing/turn-taking, concurrent actions, actions that truly require separate time, and the purpose of any remainder. Exclude punctuation from Chinese character estimates. Aim for natural conversational breaths of roughly 0.2–0.5 seconds; explain how a speech-free interval over about 0.5 seconds advances information or evidence, or merge, add permitted useful content or rebudget. These are planning/review aids, not precise model guarantees; never force overlapping speech, rushed words or accelerated source audio to meet them. A closed mouth identifies the non-speaker; it does not instruct additional waiting.
